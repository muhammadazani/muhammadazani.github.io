Current project

PhD research · The University of Western Australia · 2020–present

A systematic framework for context- and domain-driven event abstraction in multi-modal data within online collaborative learning environments

Learning platforms record every click, submission and forum post, but these low-level events say little on their own about whether a student is planning, monitoring or reflecting. My doctoral research develops a framework that abstracts raw, multi-modal event logs into high-level learning actions grounded in self-regulated learning (SRL) theory and the course context. The resulting action sequences are analysed with process mining to reveal how students regulate their learning over time, and are validated through expert judgement.

feedback and nudges

Research questions

  1. How can low-level learning events be abstracted into learning actions that are meaningful from an SRL perspective?
  2. How can course context and domain knowledge guide that abstraction across different learning environments?
  3. What do the resulting process models reveal about how students regulate their learning in collaborative online settings?
